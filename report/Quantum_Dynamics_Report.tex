\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Simulating the time evolution of interacting many-body quantum systems is exponentially expensive on classical hardware, motivating data-driven neural surrogates capable of extrapolating observable dynamics from short observation windows. In this work, we study the $N = 10$ qubit PXP Rydberg-blockade Hamiltonian across $D = 55$ continuous physical observables ($10$ local Pauli-$Z$ magnetizations and $45$ pairwise correlations) over $400$ trajectories of $1001$ steps ($\Delta t = 0.02\text{ s}$). Following an incremental \emph{``Start Small''} methodology, we first validate a rapid pilot on $20$ trajectories and investigate dimensionality reduction via a Lab~6 Autoencoder ($55 \to 16 \to 55$) coupled with a latent Transformer, showing that while latent forecasting cuts parameter count by $60.5\%$, the autoencoder reconstruction floor ($\text{MSE}_{\text{AE}} = 3.62 \times 10^{-4}$) limits accuracy on weak long-range correlations. Scaling to the full dataset in native physical space with a $\tanh$ output bound, we develop and benchmark four sequence architectures across $24$ hyperparameter runs---a direct one-shot RNN (\textbf{M1}), an autoregressive Seq2Seq RNN (\textbf{M2}), an encoder-only one-shot Transformer (\textbf{M3}), and a causal encoder--decoder Transformer (\textbf{M4})---trained with a unified three-phase loop (\emph{Teacher Forcing} $\to$ \emph{Masked Modeling} $\to$ \emph{Scheduled Sampling}). Across 3-seed Student-$t$ validation, 5-fold trajectory cross-validation, and blind test evaluation, the compact one-shot Transformer \textbf{M3 (HP3)} achieves the best overall performance ($\text{Test MSE} = \mathbf{1.21 \times 10^{-4}}$, $\text{MAE} = \mathbf{7.85 \times 10^{-3}}$, $\text{MSE}_{\text{mag}} = \mathbf{3.60 \times 10^{-5}}$), outperforming the best RNN \textbf{M1 (HP1)} ($\text{Test MSE} = 1.61 \times 10^{-4}$) by $25\%$ with zero violations of two-body quantum density positivity ($P(s_i, s_j) \ge 0$). Finally, we resolve all three project challenges: (1) aligned-target context scaling ($T_{\text{in}} \in \{5, 10, 20, 40\}$) shows $T_{\text{in}}=10$ is sufficient for competitive forecasting while M3 scales down to $\text{Test MSE} = 1.11 \times 10^{-4}$ at $T_{\text{in}}=40$; (2) temporal super-resolution ($\times 2$, $\Delta t = 0.04 \to 0.02\text{ s}$) reconstructs fine-grained dynamics with $\text{Test MSE} = 1.21 \times 10^{-4}$; and (3) multi-head decoder attention maps confirm exact causal masking ($\max_{j>i}|\alpha_{ij}| = 0$) and reveal physical specialization between boundary phase-locking and PXP quantum-scar periodicity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

Quantum many-body systems lie at the forefront of modern physics and quantum computing, as highlighted by recent Nobel Prizes in Physics (2022, 2023, 2025) and Chemistry/Machine Learning (2024). However, simulating the exact unitary evolution of an $N$-qubit interacting system requires tracking a Hilbert space of dimension $2^N$, which becomes computationally intractable on classical supercomputers beyond a few dozen qubits or over extended time horizons. Beyond this threshold, the quantum simulator effectively behaves as a black box.

Deep learning offers a compelling data-driven alternative~\cite{carleo2017}: by training sequence models on observable trajectories collected from tractable short-time regimes, neural networks can learn the effective non-local dynamical laws governing magnetization and correlation waves and extrapolate the future evolution of the system. In this project, we investigate the $N = 10$ qubit \textbf{PXP model} describing Rydberg atom arrays under nearest-neighbor excitation blockade~\cite{bernien2017}, which exhibits non-ergodic periodic revivals known as \emph{quantum many-body scars}. At each discrete timestep, the system state is characterized by $D = 55$ continuous expectation values: $10$ single-qubit magnetizations $\langle \sigma_z^i \rangle$ and $45$ two-body correlations $\langle \sigma_z^i \sigma_z^j \rangle$ ($1 \le i < j \le 10$).

In addition to building a leak-free, reproducible forecasting benchmark comparing Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM/GRU)~\cite{sutskever2014} and Attention-based Transformers~\cite{vaswani2017}, our study addresses three core scientific and engineering challenges:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Challenge 1 --- Minimizing the Input Time Window:} Determining the shortest historical observation window $T_{\text{in}} \in \{5, 10, 20, 40\}$ that still guarantees accurate multi-step forecasting over $T_{\text{out}} = 40$ future steps, evaluated on strictly aligned future targets.
    \item \textbf{Challenge 2 --- Temporal Super-Resolution ($\times 2$):} Reconstructing native high-frequency future dynamics ($\Delta t = 0.02\text{ s}$, $T_{\text{out}} = 40$) from a $2\times$ subsampled historical observation window ($\Delta t_{\text{sub}} = 0.04\text{ s}$, $T_{\text{in}} = 20$).
    \item \textbf{Challenge 3 --- Physical Interpretability via Attention Maps:} Extracting and analyzing multi-head self- and cross-attention weights from the causal Transformer decoder to explain how the network attends to past quantum oscillations when predicting future steps.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Dataset, Physical Validation \& Windowing Pipeline}

\subsection{Dataset Structure and Quantum Physical Sanity Checks}
The raw dataset (\texttt{trajectories.csv}, $\sim 464\text{ MB}$) contains $N_{\text{traj}} = 400$ independent trajectories of the $N = 10$ qubit PXP Hamiltonian, sampled uniformly at $\Delta t = 0.02\text{ s}$ from $t = 0.00\text{ s}$ to $T_{\text{fin}} = 20.00\text{ s}$, yielding $T = 1001$ time steps per trajectory ($400,400$ total rows) without any missing (\texttt{NaN} or \texttt{Inf}) values. Each time step consists of $D = 55$ observables:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}(t) = \bigl[\, m_1(t), \dots, m_{10}(t), \; c_{1,2}(t), c_{1,3}(t), \dots, c_{9,10}(t) \,\bigr]^\top \in [-1, 1]^{55},
    \label{eq:state_vector}
\end{equation}
where $m_i(t) = \langle \sigma_z^i(t) \rangle$ denotes the local Pauli-$Z$ magnetization of qubit $i$, and $c_{ij}(t) = \langle \sigma_z^i(t)\sigma_z^j(t) \rangle$ ($i < j$) denotes the two-body spatial correlation. To eliminate CSV parsing overhead ($\sim 25\text{ s}$) in downstream experiments, the dataset is serialized into a contiguous \texttt{float32} binary tensor \texttt{data/cache/trajectories.npy} of shape $(400, 1001, 55)$ ($88.1\text{ MB}$), which loads in $< 0.1\text{ s}$.

Before model development, we verified two fundamental quantum-mechanical constraints across all $400,400$ time steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Two-body reduced density matrix positivity:} For every pair of qubits $(i, j)$ and spin outcomes $s_i, s_j \in \{-1, +1\}$, the joint measurement probability must be non-negative:
    \begin{equation}
        P(s_i, s_j) = \frac{1 + s_i m_i + s_j m_j + s_i s_j c_{ij}}{4} \ge 0.
        \label{eq:quantum_positivity}
    \end{equation}
    Across all $400 \times 1001 \times 45 \times 4 = 72,072,000$ checks, the minimum observed probability is $P_{\min} = 0.1553 > 0$ with \textbf{zero violations}, confirming exact column ordering and sign consistency.
    \item \textbf{Initial product-state factorization ($t = 0$):} At $t=0$, trajectories are initialized close to unentangled product states where $c_{ij}(0) \approx m_i(0)m_j(0)$, confirmed empirically by $\mathrm{mean}\,|c_{ij}(0) - m_i(0)m_j(0)| = 0.0158$ (max $0.080$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Physical Bound and Empirical Normalization Ablation}
Because all $55$ features are Pauli expectation values, they are strictly bounded within $[-1, 1]$ on a shared physical scale. However, their amplitudes vary by spatial separation: nearest-neighbor correlations ($c_{i,i+1}$) oscillate strongly up to $\pm 0.25$, whereas distant correlations (e.g., $c_{1,10}$) remain near zero with standard deviation $\sim 10^{-3}$.

To rigorously decide whether to standardize the features, we trained a baseline recurrent model across three independent seeds ($42, 43, 44$) comparing raw physical inputs (\texttt{none}) with a final $\tanh$ activation against column-wise Z-score standardization (\texttt{zscore}) with a linear output head, evaluating both on the original physical scale (Table~\ref{tab:norm_ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:data_and_norm}b).

Rescaling via Z-score amplifies numerical noise on near-flat long-range correlations by orders of magnitude, distorting dot-product attention scores in Transformers, while the $\tanh$ output layer naturally enforces the exact $[-1, 1]$ quantum bound and prevents unbounded drift during closed-loop autoregressive rollouts.

\subsection{Anti-Leakage Trajectory Split and Sliding-Window Extraction}
Splitting a time-series dataset by row would assign overlapping segments of the same trajectory to both training and test sets, causing severe temporal data leakage. We therefore partition the dataset \emph{per trajectory} (\texttt{SEED = 42}) into \textbf{280 training (70\%)}, \textbf{60 validation (15\%)}, and \textbf{60 test (15\%)} trajectories, frozen in \texttt{results/split\_indices.npz} ($\mathcal{T}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{T}_{\text{val}} \cap \mathcal{T}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$).

Sliding windows are extracted strictly within individual trajectories using a vectorized stride trick ($320.6 \pm 82.1\text{ ms}$ for the entire dataset, $>83,850\text{ windows/s}$). All main benchmark models share a fixed forecasting horizon $T_{\text{out}} = 40$ steps ($0.80\text{ s}$) and stride $s = 10$ steps ($0.20\text{ s}$) across two input window configurations (Table~\ref{tab:windows} and Fig.~\ref{fig:window_samples}).

\subsection{Incremental ``Start Small'' Pilot and Lab 6 Autoencoder Reduction}
Following the course guidelines (\emph{``Start Small: begin with a limited subset of data, short windows, and compact models; explore dimensionality reduction via an Autoencoder''}), we conducted two fast preliminary studies on a $20$-trajectory training subset ($1,920$ windows) and $10$ validation trajectories ($960$ windows) on the \texttt{short} window ($T_{\text{in}}=10$) for $5$ epochs ($2\text{ TF} + 2\text{ MM} + 1\text{ SS}$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Micro-Transformer Pilot:} Compact versions of \textbf{M3} and \textbf{M4} ($d_{\text{model}}=32, H=2, d_{\text{ff}}=64, L=1$) converge in $5.0\text{ s}$ and $23.4\text{ s}$, respectively, confirming pipeline correctness.

\textbf{Key Insight:} Operating in the $16$-dimensional latent space reduces Transformer parameters by $\mathbf{60.5\%}$ ($34,400$ vs.\ $87,128$) and slightly improves 5-epoch pilot accuracy ($7.37 \times 10^{-4}$ vs.\ $7.52 \times 10^{-4}$). \noindent
\textbf{Architectural Details (Lab~5 \& Lab~7 Compliance):}
All models are built strictly from modular building blocks introduced in the course laboratories:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{M1 (\texttt{RNNDirect}) \& M2 (\texttt{RNNSeq2Seq}):} Built with cuDNN-accelerated \texttt{LSTM} or \texttt{GRU} layers ($d_{\text{lat}} = 64$, $L \in \{1, 2\}$). In \textbf{M1}, the final hidden state $\mathbf{h}_{T_{\text{in}}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\text{lat}}}$ is mapped by $\text{Dense}(T_{\text{out}} \cdot D, \tanh)$ and reshaped to $(B, T_{\text{out}}, D)$. In \textbf{M2}, the encoder states $[(\mathbf{h}_l, \mathbf{c}_l)]_{l=1}^L$ initialize a matching stack of decoder recurrent layers that generate one token $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = \tanh(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{h}_t^{\text{dec}} + \mathbf{b}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1 \times D}$ per step during closed-loop \texttt{free\_running} inference.
    \item \textbf{M3 (\texttt{TransformerDirect}) \& M4 (\texttt{TransformerSeq2Seq}):} Follow the exact modular hierarchy of Lab~7 (\texttt{Transformer.ipynb}):
    \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt]
        \item \texttt{PositionalEmbedding}: Replaces the discrete NLP token \texttt{Embedding} with a continuous linear projection $\text{Dense}(d_{\text{model}})$ scaled by $\sqrt{d_{\text{model}}}$ and summed with the deterministic sinusoidal \texttt{positional\_encoding} matrix over geometric frequencies $1/10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}$.
        \item \texttt{BaseAttention} subclasses: \texttt{GlobalSelfAttention} (bidirectional over $T_{\text{in}}$ in the Encoder), \texttt{CausalSelfAttention} (\texttt{use\_causal\_mask=True} in the Decoder to block future leakage $j > i$), and \texttt{CrossAttention} (Decoder queries attending to Encoder keys/values, caching \texttt{last\_attn\_scores} of shape $(B, H, T_{\text{out}}, T_{\text{in}})$). Each block applies Post-LN residual connections (\texttt{Add} + \texttt{LayerNormalization}) and a point-wise \texttt{FeedForward} network ($\text{Dense}(d_{\text{ff}}, \text{ReLU}) \to \text{Dense}(d_{\text{model}}) \to \text{Dropout}$).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unified Three-Phase Hybrid Training Loop}
All models are trained for $25$ epochs using a shared three-phase curriculum (\texttt{src/training.py}, compiled with \texttt{@tf.function(reduce\_retracing=True)}, Adam optimizer with $\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$, batch size $B = 64$, MSE loss, and best-checkpoint restoration on realistic validation MSE):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Phase 1 --- Teacher Forcing (TF, Epochs 1--10):} For Seq2Seq models (M2, M4), the decoder receives the ground-truth target shifted right by one step, anchored at $t=0$ by the last observed encoder state: $Y_{\text{dec}}[:, 0] = X[:, -1]$ and $Y_{\text{dec}}[:, 1:] = Y[:, :-1]$. Provides clean gradients for rapid initial convergence.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2 --- Masked Modeling (MM, Epochs 11--20):} A random fraction $p_{\text{mask}} = 0.15$ of input timesteps in $X$ (and $Y_{\text{dec}}$ for Seq2Seq) is zeroed out while protecting the boundary anchor token ($X[:, -1]$ and $Y_{\text{dec}}[:, 0]$). Forces the network to infer internal physical phase from surrounding context rather than relying on local copying.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3 --- Scheduled Sampling (SS, Epochs 21--25):} Following Bengio et al.~\cite{bengio2015} and the parallel two-pass Transformer scheme~\cite{mihaylova2019}, a first stop-gradient forward pass generates predictions $\hat{Y}$; then each decoder input token for $t \ge 1$ is sampled from $\hat{Y}_{t-1}$ with probability $p_k$ ramping linearly from $p_{\text{start}} = 0.0$ to $p_{\text{end}} = 0.5$, bridging the distribution gap between assisted teacher forcing and open-loop inference.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Discussion of Main Findings:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Why M3 (\texttt{TransformerDirect}) is the overall winner:} M3 (HP3) reduces global Test MSE by $\mathbf{25\%}$ compared to the best RNN M1 ($1.21 \times 10^{-4}$ vs.\ $1.61 \times 10^{-4}$) and reduces single-qubit magnetization error by $\mathbf{50\%}$ ($\text{MSE}_{\text{mag}} = 3.60 \times 10^{-5}$ vs.\ $7.20 \times 10^{-5}$). Unlike an LSTM, which compresses the input window sequentially into a single state vector, \texttt{GlobalSelfAttention} computes direct pairwise interactions across all past timesteps before projecting the contextualized representation onto the future horizon.
    \item \textbf{Why compact HP3 wins on M3 while deeper HP1/HP2 win on M4/M2:} For one-shot projection (M3), a single unregularized attention block ($L=1, H=2$, $\text{dropout}=0.0$) avoids random dropout noise on deterministic Hamiltonian trajectories and trains in half the time ($73\text{ s}$ vs.\ $132\text{ s}$). Conversely, step-by-step autoregressive decoders (M2, M4) require $L=2$ layers with $\text{dropout}=0.1$ (HP2 for M2, HP1 for M4) to regularize against self-generated noise during closed-loop rollouts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions \& Future Work}
% =============================================================================

We presented an end-to-end deep learning framework for forecasting the 55-observable dynamics of a 10-qubit PXP quantum simulator. Our experiments demonstrate that: (i) preserving raw physical observables in $[-1, 1]$ with a $\tanh$ output head outperforms Z-score standardization and guarantees $100\%$ physical density-matrix positivity ($P(s_i,s_j) \ge 0$); (ii) one-shot direct architectures (\textbf{M3}, $\text{Test MSE} = 1.21 \times 10^{-4}$; \textbf{M1}, $\text{Test MSE} = 1.61 \times 10^{-4}$) eliminate autoregressive exposure bias and outperform closed-loop Seq2Seq decoders (\textbf{M2}, \textbf{M4}); and (iii) self-attention unlocks superior context scaling down to $\text{Test MSE} = 1.11 \times 10^{-4}$ at $T_{\text{in}}=40$ (Challenge~1), lossless $\times 2$ temporal super-resolution at $\text{Test MSE} = 1.21 \times 10^{-4}$ (Challenge~2), and physically interpretable attention maps (Challenge~3).

\noindent
\textbf{Future Directions:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Direct--Autoregressive Block Rollouts:} Chaining M3 one-shot blocks of length $T_{\text{out}}=40$ iteratively to forecast ultra-long horizons ($T_{\text{out}} \ge 160$) while limiting autoregressive feedback steps by $40\times$.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Informed Positivity Regularization:} Incorporating soft penalty terms on the eigenvalues of the two-body reduced density matrix for extreme subsampling factors ($\times 4, \times 8$).
    \item \textbf{Cross-System Size Generalization ($N > 10$):} Replacing the fixed-$D$ input projection with graph/spatial attention over qubit coordinates to zero-shot extrapolate from $N=10$ to larger Rydberg chains ($N \ge 20$).
\end{itemize}
